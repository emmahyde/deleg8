Architecture — four new parts, one existing spine

new in deleg8 exists today append-only store omp agent process browser view

The orchestrator spawns one seat per model — heterogeneous providers, capped at 6 concurrent omp processes. Seats never message each other directly: they bid for a floor, and the winner appends to a ledger. Everything the office renders is a projection of that ledger, so the browser is a pure view and closing it does not stop the panel. Two return paths are omitted to keep the chart readable: the Ledger feeds back into the Visibility policy on every append, and mirrors each event to the orchestrator as a channel notification.

Behavioral triggers — why a seat speaks, and the two gates that keep it quiet

new mechanism deliberate suppression recorded to ledger

A seat never decides to speak — it decides to bid. Bidding is cheap and bounded; speaking is not. The two pink gates exist because the measured failure mode of a shared LLM channel is not chaos, it is collapse: everyone agreeing with whoever spoke first.

Standup lifecycle — blind draft first, reveal second

new phase decision recorded real work, real tools

The ordering is the whole design. Every seat commits its take on the item before seeing any peer's take, and the reveal order is randomized per item. Anchoring needs a first speaker; a blind draft denies it one. Only after the reveal does the room become reactive, and that reactive phase is bounded. Two return paths are omitted from the chart: Work re-enters the reactive round when a seat's tool_activity is worth surfacing, and falls back to idle when the item is closed.

Data model — what the ledger actually stores

Ledger — append-only Casting — seat identity Floor — turn control Stage — view projection

One append-only event table carries the room; everything else is either identity or a projection. room_events is the only durable truth — seat state, floor state, and the office scene are all derivable by replay, which is what makes the browser disposable.

Notes

  • New is everything under Casting, Floor, Ledger, and Stage. None of it exists on main today. The extension point is HOST_TOOLS at src/server.ts:317 plus a matching branch in handleHostRequest at src/server.ts:403 — the same seam msg, task_create, and exclusive_acquire already use.
  • Reused, not rebuilt: the Floor is a cooperative lock, and deleg8 already ships one. exclusive_acquire / exclusive_release (src/server.ts:367) blocks until granted and is already understood by spawned agents. The floor is that lock keyed on room:<id>:floor.
  • The two pink gates are the point of the design. A shared transcript plus per-seat personas is the exact condition measured to produce strongest majority dominance (arXiv:2510.02360, Spiral of Silence). Inter-agent sycophancy scores below a single-agent baseline (arXiv:2509.23055). Build the room without these gates and it looks alive while behaving as one model with extra latency.
  • Blind draft is not a nicety. The same Spiral of Silence work isolates history as the anchoring signal — persona alone produced "diverse but uncorrelated opinions." Removing peer history for the first pass is the cheapest available defense, and it matches the All-Agents Drafting result that improved task performance in arXiv:2502.19130.
  • Bounded rounds, deliberately. The same paper measures that more discussion rounds before a decision reduce performance. R is a hard cap, not a timeout.
  • Heterogeneity has a cost the diagram cannot show. Agents align with the numerically dominant group or the more capable model, over 2,500 measured debates (arXiv:2506.01332). A panel of Opus + two smaller models will drift toward Opus. Seat composition is a tuning parameter, not decoration.
  • Deliberately omitted: agent-to-agent direct messaging. There is no whisper. Every utterance is on the record, because a side channel reintroduces exactly the invisible influence the gates exist to bound.
  • Assumption, not yet confirmed: the office is a view over authoritative server-side state, not a spatial simulation. Seats do not have look or move tools and proximity does not gate who hears whom. Reversing this is a large change — it would feed space back into the prompts.
  • Graphics is not the constraint. First-hand profiling puts the animated-SkinnedMesh cliff at 200–300 characters on 2014 hardware; DELEG8_MAX_AGENTS defaults to 6 (src/server.ts:572). Roughly two orders of magnitude of headroom. Naive per-seat SkinnedMesh + AnimationMixer is correct; instanced skinning is not worth its complexity here.
  • Character pipeline avoids a dead vendor. Ready Player Me shut down public access 2026-01-31 after the Netflix acquisition, and it is the subject of most existing tutorials. Standardize instead on the mixamorig* bone-naming contract; VRM/VRoid, Microsoft RocketBox (MIT), and MPFB (CC0) all conform, so one animation set drives every seat through a small bone-name remap.
  • Weakest-evidenced element: the Stage's fixed-tick + interpolation-buffer transport. No corpus source covers websocket/SSE-driven three.js reconciliation directly; it is reasoned by analogy from Crowd.update's interpolatedPosition in recast-navigation-js. Treat it as the first thing to validate, not as settled.
