## Research Brief

**Question:** What is the current (2025-2026) state of the art for sourcing, rigging, animating, and giving distinct speaking behavior to stylized humanoid three.js characters representing AI agents in a shared virtual office?

**Opinion:** Ready Player Me is dead as a foundation (do not build on it). Build instead on a **Mixamo-compatible bone-name/blendshape contract** — mix VRM (VRoid Studio), MIT-licensed Microsoft RocketBox humans, and CC0 MPFB procedural characters as the N distinct meshes, share one Mixamo animation set across all of them via bone-name remapping (not a dedicated retargeting library — none exists for three.js), hand-roll a thin FSM over `THREE.AnimationMixer` with manual weight-lerp crossfades (not `crossFadeTo`, which is unreliable in practice), and adopt **met4citizen/TalkingHead** wholesale (or its architecture) for gesture loops, idle/talk procedural motion, and Oculus/ARKit viseme lip-sync — driven by text timing alone if no TTS budget exists. Skip instanced skinning entirely; it's unmerged, DIY-only tech in three.js and unnecessary below roughly a hundred simultaneous characters.

**Confidence:** Medium — High for the RPM-death and viseme-standard findings (multiple independent, dated, primary sources agree); Medium for the retargeting and FSM conclusions (strong primary evidence but derived from absence-of-a-better-option rather than a definitive comparison); Low for the character-distinctness and Quaternius/Kenney/Meshy sourcing questions (search budget did not surface direct evidence).

**Reasoning:**
The initial hypothesis was that Ready Player Me + Mixamo + a dedicated three.js retargeting library would be the standard 2026 stack. Evidence broke the first half: RPM was acquired by Netflix in Dec 2025 and shut down public developer access Jan 31, 2026 (01-en-wikipedia-org-wiki-ready-player-me.md), so it is a trap for new builds despite being the subject of nearly every older tutorial found (09-wawasensei, 08-talkinghead's own example asset). The retargeting half held up differently than expected: there is no dedicated three.js retargeting library in the corpus; instead, every real implementation (08-talkinghead, 13-gabber-dev) achieves cross-character animation sharing by keeping to one bone-naming convention (Mixamo's `mixamorig*` prefix, auto-stripped by TalkingHead) or by hand-writing a bone-name-to-bone-name dictionary and rebuilding `AnimationClip` tracks under new names (13-gabber-dev's `MIXAMO_VRM_RIG_MAP`). discoverthreejs's own animation-system chapter independently confirms the mechanism: a clip is tied to track names, so it "should work with other characters from mixamo.com" (same bone names) but is "unlikely to work with just any humanoid model" (04-discoverthreejs). For the state-machine question, `AnimationMixer`/`AnimationAction` is explicitly a low-level "mixing desk," not a state machine (04-discoverthreejs), and a practitioner report shows the built-in `crossFadeTo` snapping to a T-pose instead of blending, forcing a hand-rolled linear weight-lerp workaround (05-discourse-crossfadeto). For speaking behavior, met4citizen/TalkingHead is corroborated across three sources as the maintained, purpose-built solution (08-talkinghead's own README plus its citation in a Jan-2026 production tutorial, 13-gabber-dev, which uses the same Oculus-viseme + Mixamo-retarget pattern independently) and is validated by real deployments through 2025 (a CHI 2025 paper, a Cannes AI Film Award feature). The Oculus (15 visemes) and ARKit (52 blendshapes) standards recur in every lip-sync source (08, 10-vrchat-visemes, 12-convai), confirming they are the actual cross-engine contract, not competing options. For performance, the only primary evidence (06-discourse-instanced-skinning) is a single detailed practitioner thread showing instanced skinning is unmerged, DIY, and driven by bone-texture upload cost rather than triangle count — good evidence for the mechanism, but a sample size of one project.

**Key evidence:**
- Ready Player Me acquired by Netflix Dec 2025, shut down public/developer service Jan 31 2026 — challenges the "RPM is a safe base" assumption — `pages/01-en-wikipedia-org-wiki-ready-player-me.md`
- Mixamo raw animation files "can't be distributed outside the project team and can't be used to train ML models," but retail royalty-free use is fine — bounds what a self-hosted animation library may redistribute — `pages/08-github-com-met4citizen-talkinghead.md`
- Cross-character animation sharing in the wild is done via bone-name remap dictionaries (`MIXAMO_VRM_RIG_MAP`) rebuilding `QuaternionKeyframeTrack`s, not a dedicated retargeting library — supports "hand-roll the remap" conclusion — `pages/13-gabber-dev-blog-build-a-threejs-3d-avatar-with-realtime-ai-v.md`
- discoverthreejs: a clip "should work with other characters from mixamo.com" (shared bone names) but "unlikely to work with just any humanoid model you download" — independently corroborates the bone-name-contract mechanism — `pages/04-discoverthreejs-com-book-first-steps-animation-system.md`
- `AnimationAction.crossFadeTo` snaps to T-pose instead of blending in a live practitioner repro; custom linear weight-lerp function fixes it — challenges reliance on stock crossfade API — `pages/05-discourse-threejs-org-t-animationaction-crossfadeto-not-work.md`
- TalkingHead requires "a Mixamo-compatible rig and ARKit and Oculus viseme blend shapes," supports text-driven `speakText` with no TTS/audio required (`avatarMute` option), and is used in a CHI 2025 paper and a 2025 Cannes AI Film Award-featured app — supports "adopt TalkingHead, audio optional" — `pages/08-github-com-met4citizen-talkinghead.md`
- Oculus/OVR Lip Sync (15 visemes, real-time, "easy to implement" but limited) sits below NVIDIA Audio2Face (52 ARKit blendshapes, AI-driven, higher quality but slower, enterprise) on the cost/complexity ladder — supports picking the cheaper Oculus-viseme tier for many simultaneous cheap characters — `pages/12-home-convai-com-blog-lip-syncing-virtual-ai-characters-techn.md`
- Instanced skinned meshes are not native to three.js core; a practitioner's custom `SkinnedInstancer` bakes bone matrices into a shared texture atlas and adds bone-count LOD (67 bones/1MB texture at LOD0 down to 14 bones/0.0625MB at LOD1) to control GPU upload cost — supports "skip instancing below ~100 characters, LOD bones not just geometry if scaling up" — `pages/06-discourse-threejs-org-t-animated-instanced-skinned-meshes-gl.md`
- Microsoft RocketBox (MIT licensed, ready-made humans, needs re-rigging via Mixamo) and MPFB (CC0/CC-BY, free parametric Blender human generator) are named as compatible, low-cost character sources for the shared-rig approach — supports the "mix free sources under one rig contract" distinctness strategy — `pages/08-github-com-met4citizen-talkinghead.md`

**Conflicts / unknowns:**
- No conflict found between sources on the viseme standards or on RPM's shutdown — all corroborating.
- Genuinely open: whether a dedicated three.js animation-FSM library exists that beats hand-rolling one. The corpus search (query 2) surfaced only agent-skill-listicle sites (agentskills.me, claudeskills.club, agentpedia.codes) referencing "threejs-animation" skills, not actual FSM libraries; these were excluded from the fetch corpus as low-quality/SEO and the gap was not closed by a second search (budget was fixed at 3 searches for this Deep-tier question). Treat "hand-roll the FSM" as the current best answer, not a proven absence of alternatives.
- Genuinely open: Quaternius, Kenney, and Meshy/AI-3D-generation were named in the task but did not surface in the Phase 1 search results at all (query 1 returned RPM/VRoid/Mixamo content only) — the corpus has zero direct evidence on these three sources' 2026 licensing or maintenance status.

**Would change this opinion:** A follow-up search turning up (a) a maintained three.js-specific animation state-machine package (e.g. an XState/three.js integration) with real adoption, which would replace "hand-roll the FSM," or (b) current licensing pages for Quaternius/Kenney/Meshy confirming or denying their fitness as one of the N distinct-character sources, which would let them compete with RocketBox/MPFB/VRoid in the distinctness recommendation.
